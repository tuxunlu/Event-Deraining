## Protocols — read this before comparing any two numbers

| protocol | what it is | comparable to |
|---|---|---|
| **KITTI 50-epoch** | train on 14 rain rates, select τ on val {20,80} mm, report on test {50,150} mm | other KITTI 50-epoch numbers only |
| **KITTI 10-epoch triage** | val DA, `T_max=10` cosine schedule | other 10-epoch arms only |
| **SPAC** | scene-disjoint: train t1–t8 / val a1–a4 / test b1–b4 | other SPAC numbers only |

Three traps that have already produced wrong conclusions in this project:

1. **Triage overstates every gain by 3–5×** (fig8, left). Never quote a 10-epoch number as a result.
2. **The 10- and 50-epoch runs use different LR schedules**, so a 10-epoch arm is not comparable to the 50-epoch run's epoch-10 value.
3. **Seed noise is ±0.0039** (3 seeds, ORSPNet). Most differences in this project are inside it. Every figure draws the band.
4. **Pixel DA does not predict event DA, because of the GT definition** (the
   most expensive trap in the project — see the PRE-Mamba section). Every model
   on the leaderboard is trained and scored against `gt[y,x] = 1 iff ≥1 clean
   **ON** event lands in that pixel`, an OR-union inherited from the ON-only
   eFFT pipeline. Measured on KITTI test, that target's own ceiling in
   event-level accounting is **0.6981** — a *perfect* model of it discards
   ~48% of background events (all OFF signal events are unrepresented, SR
   0.5215). The leaderboard is internally consistent, so DFFN vs ORSPNet vs
   FourierMamba vs EvORSP-3T remain comparable, but no pixel-DA number in this
   README should be read as predicting per-event deraining quality.

Latency is measured on an **idle** GPU, batch 1, 256×256, 100 warm-up iterations with `cudnn.benchmark`, 7 repeats, median. Training wall-clock is not latency; FLOPs are not latency.

**Where EvORSP-3T's win actually comes from (fig2, fig4):** almost entirely rain *removal*. Its NR is 0.967 / 0.965 at 50 / 150 mm against ~0.91–0.93 for every 2D model — the red "rain kept" pixels visibly vanish from its error-map column — while its SR (0.958 / 0.896) stays inside the pack. The temporal+OFF input identifies rain events; it does not recover extra signal. That is exactly what a persistence prior should do: rain is brief and one-sided in polarity, so restoring those two axes makes rain *recognisable*, not the scene more visible.

## Headline numbers

**Best configuration: ORSPNet + dil at 7.13 ms, 36,782 params, test DA 0.9248** — with the operating-point correction applied by any of three routes. Against DFFN: **+0.0089 DA, 1.66× faster, half the parameters.**

Three routes to the same correction land on the same number, which is the point:

| route | when applied | test DA |
|---|---|---|
| balanced loss | training | 0.9248 |
| prior threshold τ = π̂ | inference, zero cost, no tuning | 0.9248 |
| lit-masked BCE | training | 0.9244 |

They are the same correction implemented three ways. Stacking them hurts — the learned rate→logit bias measured **−0.0025** on top of the balanced loss.

## What did and did not move the metric

- **Receptive field is the only architectural lever that beat noise**: +0.0055 test DA for **576 parameters** (dil, RF 33→129 px).
- **Capacity does not help**: DFFN has 2× the parameters of ORSPNet and scores 0.0034 *lower*.
- **Orientation is load-bearing**: making the atom bank isotropic costs **−0.0082**, over 2× seed noise (fig8, right). This is the evidence against the reading that ORSPNet is "LSKNet with an analytic basis".
- **Total spread across every architectural change tried: 0.0109.** The frontend explains why — the network input is verified to be a **1-bit ON-occupancy mask** (OFF events and counts discarded), so all models read the same one bit per pixel.
- **The temporal axis is worth more than any operator change** (fig9, fig10). Separability is AUC **0.7265 at τ = 5 ms** but only **0.5549** at the 104 ms accumulation window we use. Single-pixel timing is chance at every scale, so the signal is spatio*temporal*, not temporal.

### 3D log-Gabor on SPAC — the controlled test (fig10)

T is the only variable; T=1 is the bit-identical OR-union of the T=16 planes.

| T | params | test DA | vs 2D baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (2D baseline) | 36,782 | 0.7405 | — |
| 4 | 36,952 | **0.7835** | +0.0430 |
| 8 | 36,952 | 0.7651 | +0.0246 |
| 16 | 36,952 | 0.7735 | +0.0330 |

**Every temporal arm beats the 2D baseline, by +0.0246 to +0.0430 (mean +0.0335), for +170 parameters and +0.17 ms.** That is ~8× the best architectural gain on KITTI (dil, +0.0055) at 3.4× fewer added parameters.

The ordering *among* T is **not** resolved: the spread between temporal arms is 0.0184 and non-monotonic (T=4 > T=16 > T=8), which is consistent with run-to-run variation at one seed per arm. Do not read a trend into the choice of T from this table; 3 seeds are needed.

### The input-stack sweep and the final model (fig11)

Building on T=4, two frontend levers and a depth cut were tested with everything else held fixed. Latency: idle node, batch 1, 256×256, 100 warm-up, median of 7, all spreads ≤ 0.06 ms.

| config | params | ms | test DA (seeds) | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T=4 control (ON-only, 4 blocks) | 36,952 | 7.27 | .7835 .7818 .7825 | 0.7826 ± 0.0009 |
| b3 (ON-only) | 27,855 | 5.54 | .7812 | 0.7812 |
| **EvORSP-3T** (b3 + OFF) | 28,060 | **5.69** | .8171 .8274 .8132 | **0.8192 ± 0.0073** |
| + counts | 28,142 | 5.71 | .8245 .8337 .8033 | 0.8205 ± 0.0155 |
| b4 + OFF | 37,157 | 7.45 | .8377 | 0.8377 |
| b4 + OFF + counts | 37,239 | 7.43 | .8275 | 0.8275 |
| b2 + OFF + counts | 19,045 | 4.10 | .8057 | 0.8057 |

**Headline: the 3-block OFF-channel model beats the previous best by +0.037 (≈5σ of its own seed std) while 22 % faster and 24 % smaller.** The OFF channel is the accuracy engine (+0.037–0.054, consistent across four configs); the block cut is nearly free without OFF (−0.0023) because latency is dominated by memory-bound elementwise work that scales with block count.

**Verdicts from pre-registered gates** (each number was stated before the experiment ran):
- **Count channels: noise-level.** +0.0074 / −0.0102 across single-seed pairs; 3-seed means 0.8205 (with) vs 0.8192 (without) — 0.1σ apart. The temporal frontend already encodes the persistence signal the counts carry. Retained in the "+ counts" config only by the letter of the selection rule; droppable at zero cost.
- **Spectral gate fold: dead.** Saved 0.065 ms/block against a 0.10 kill line — the eager dilated branches are already too cheap for an FFT round-trip to beat.
- **SWA: dead.** Lost on val 3 of 3 times; the val-only selection rule dropped it each time at zero cost.
- **Seed spread honesty:** the OFF-channel arms spread ~0.007–0.016 across seeds (the ON-only control spreads only 0.0009). Single-seed differences among the OFF arms — including b4+OFF's 0.8377 — are not meaningful. Every multi-seed claim above uses means.

## Real-sensor rain (EventRain-27K `real/`, EVK4)

Zero-shot transfer of KITTI-trained models to real EVK4 rain **fails for every
model**: DA 0.516–0.550 (chance = 0.5), against 0.92+ on synthetic. EvORSP-3T
degrades least (0.5498; oracle ceiling 0.5674, the only one meaningfully above
chance). Real rain is a hard domain shift: real sensor, 1280×720 optics,
full-height streaks, 5–8× event density. Label semantics verified visually
(label 1 = scene, 0 = rain; per-event labels).

**Trained on real data** (scene-disjoint: train scenes 1–2 / val 3 / test 4,
same protocol, single seed each):

| model | input | test DA (scene 4) |
|---|---|---|
| 2D control (same body family, 4 blocks) | 1-bit | 0.7649 |
| **EvORSP-3T** | temporal + OFF | **0.8170** |

**The input advantage on real sensor data is +0.0521 — twice the synthetic-data
gain** (+0.0259 KITTI, +0.037 SPAC). Real rain's temporal/polarity structure is
richer than any renderer's, so restoring those axes pays more, not less. The
zero-shot collapse was domain shift, not a method failure; in-domain training
recovers strong performance (0.55 → 0.82), and real rain remains legitimately
harder than synthetic (0.82 vs 0.95).

## Head-to-head with PRE-Mamba (ICCV 2025) — fig13

PRE-Mamba (arXiv 2505.05307, the EventRain-27K paper) was cloned and run
locally: its **released SYTHETIC checkpoint** (264,796 params = the paper's
0.26M, best epoch 44) evaluated with **its own test harness** on **our exact
KITTI test levels {50, 150}mm**. Fairness of the frame: their training levels
{1,10,15,17,25,30,40,60,75,100,175,200} exclude 50/150 (as does ours — we
train on 14 levels, they on 12), so neither model saw the test rates. Their
metric is per-frame event-level DA = ½(background recall + rain recall) over
labeled events, frame-averaged — the event-granularity analogue of our
pixel DA.

**The 2×2 result (fig13): each model wins under its own granularity.**

| KITTI test {50,150}mm | our pixel DA (lit pixels, 256²) | their event DA |
|---|---|---|
| EvORSP-3T (28,060 params, 3 seeds) | **0.9465 ± 0.0001** | 0.7052 ± 0.0169 |
| PRE-Mamba (264,796 params, released) | 0.8754 | **0.9172** |

Per level, their event DA: 50mm 0.9329 / 150mm 0.9014. Ours under their
metric used a τ re-selected on val {20,80}mm *by event-DA* (not the pixel τ),
so the 0.7052 is not a threshold artifact. Conversely, PRE-Mamba rasterized to
the eFFT-style ON-occupancy mask (pixel kept iff ≥1 ON event kept) under-removes
at pixel level (pixel NR 0.8074): enough rain events survive to keep rain
pixels lit.

### Why we lose the event metric — it is the TARGET, not the granularity

The obvious reading of 0.7052 is a granularity ceiling: one decision per 256²
pixel must keep every event in a kept pixel, rain included. **That reading is
wrong, and measuring it was the most valuable experiment of the campaign.**

`granularity_oracle.py` computes the exact event-DA of the BA-optimal decision
at a given output granularity (keep cell iff `bg/N_bg > rain/N_rain`) — the
ceiling of *any* model deciding at that resolution:

| ceiling | T=1 | T=4 | T=8 | T=16 | T=20 | T=64 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 256² (ours) | **0.9466** | 0.9566 | 0.9685 | 0.9807 | 0.9837 | 0.9939 |
| native 460×352 | 0.9578 | 0.9688 | 0.9814 | 0.9906 | 0.9925 | 0.9976 |

Our own granularity permits **0.9466** — *above* PRE-Mamba's 0.9172. Rain and
signal co-locate within a 100 ms window far less than assumed. So the deficit
is not granularity. `target_oracle.py` then evaluates the candidate targets:

| per-pixel decision rule (256², T=1) | SR | NR | event-DA |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — ≥1 clean ON event (our actual GT)** | 0.5215 | 0.8746 | **0.6981** |
| B — ≥1 clean event, either polarity | 1.0000 | 0.7578 | 0.8789 |
| C — background count > rain count | 0.9311 | 0.9568 | 0.9440 |
| D — BA-optimal (ceiling) | 0.9383 | 0.9550 | 0.9466 |

**EvORSP-3T achieves 0.7052 against a target whose ceiling is 0.6981** — it is
saturated, not underperforming. The ON-only OR-union target is the entire
0.212 deficit, and rule A's SR of 0.5215 names the mechanism: every OFF signal
event is structurally unlabelled, so a perfect model of this GT throws half the
background away. PRE-Mamba's advantage is a *supervision* advantage (it labels
every event of both polarities), not an architectural one.

The fix ladder is therefore data-side, not architecture-side:
polarity-complete target (→0.8789), count-majority target (→0.9440, already
above PRE-Mamba), temporal readout `out_chans=T` (→0.9807 at T=16, and
`out_chans` was **already** a constructor argument — +495 params, no
architectural work). Note that the temporal readout is also exactly PRE-Mamba's
own granularity mechanism: its `GridSample` computes
`coord / np.array((1,1,grid_size))`, dividing **only the time axis**, so its
"per-event" decisions are per-(native pixel × 20 normalized-time bins),
broadcast back to events via `return_inverse` — not per-raw-event computation.

### The retrained result (fig13) — a match, at 9.4× fewer params

`run_kitti_e.py` retrains the **identical trunk** against rule C (packs rebuilt
by `kitti_build_e.py` storing exact per-cell background/rain counts, so the
event metric is computed from counts with no raw-event pass). 50 epochs, same
optimizer/schedule, one global τ on val {20,80}mm, test {50,150}mm:

| model | params | latency | 50mm | 150mm | **test event-DA** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EvORSP-3T, old ON-only OR target | 28,060 | 5.73 ms | — | — | 0.7052 |
| **EvORSP-3T/E**, rule C, `T_out=1` (2 seeds) | 28,060 | **5.73 ms** | 0.9375 | 0.8958 | **0.9166 ± 0.0001** |
| **EvORSP-3T/E**, rule C, `T_out=16` (2 seeds) | 28,555 | **5.74 ms** | 0.9405 | 0.8962 | **0.9196 ± 0.0013** |
| PRE-Mamba (released ckpt) | 264,632 | 306 / 409 ms | 0.9329 | 0.9014 | **0.9172** |

Seeds: `T_out=1` → 0.9167, 0.9165; `T_out=16` → 0.9183, 0.9209.

**Read this as a match, not a win.** `T_out=16` is nominally +0.0024 over
PRE-Mamba, but its own two-seed range is 0.0026 — the same size as the margin.
`T_out=1` is −0.0006, i.e. a tie. The defensible claim is *equal event-level
accuracy at 9.3× fewer parameters and 53–71× lower latency*. Split by
intensity, we are better at 50mm (+0.0076) and worse at 150mm (−0.0052): their
point backbone holds up better in the heaviest rain.

Latency is the definitive idle-node measurement (all 4 GPUs at 1 MiB, load
0.27): ours 5.73/5.74 ms with spreads 0.138/0.065 ms — **the temporal readout
costs 0.01 ms** — against PRE-Mamba's 306.06 ms on a 50mm frame (599K points
gathered for 70.9K current-scan events) and 409.19 ms at 150mm, spreads 1.8 and
0.3 ms. Per *input* point that is ~0.51 s/M, consistent with the paper's
reported 0.398 s/M on an A6000; the cost is rate-dependent by construction,
ours is not.

Two honest negatives from the same runs:

- **The temporal readout is not the lever.** `T_out=16` raises the ceiling from
  0.9466 to 0.9807 but buys only **+0.0016** measured. The trunk cannot exploit
  finer temporal output; the earlier hypothesis that per-(pixel, time-bin)
  granularity was the key mechanism is not supported.
- **~0.026 of learnable headroom remains** (0.9183 achieved vs 0.9440 ceiling
  for rule C at T_out=1). After the target fix the residual gap is genuine model
  capacity, which is where a per-event head or a larger trunk would now be
  tested — not before.

Latency (provisional — measured on a contended node, definitive idle-node
re-run pending): PRE-Mamba **317 ms** on a mid-sequence 50mm test frame
(599K points into the network for the 5-scan gather) and **564 ms** at 150mm,
versus EvORSP-3T's rate-invariant **5.69 ms**. The ~0.5 s/M-points cost is
consistent with the paper's own 0.4 s/M events on stronger hardware; the
architecture is rate-dependent by construction.

Protocol notes for the paper: (1) their released config *validates on 50mm*,
so their checkpoint selection saw one of our test rates (ours selected on
{20,80} only) — a small advantage in their favor we did not correct;
(2) their tester's synthetic `data_name` omits the rain level, so 150mm
results silently overwrite 50mm in its aggregate — the numbers above are
recomputed from the intact per-frame log lines, and the pred files came from
a rerun with the name collision patched; (3) their aggregate metric
line is broken — `PreciseEvaluator` printed `SR:1.7862 || NR:1.2907 ||
DA:1.5384` (recalls above 1), a summation bug; all numbers here are recomputed
from their intact per-frame log lines.

never beat its own epoch-1 validation (best val DA 0.8076 throughout, final
evals 0.75–0.78), which suggests under-training rather than a converged result.
The released checkpoint's stored val metric (0.8157) is the fairer indicator of

real-data accuracy, at 9.4× fewer parameters**, with our synthetic-KITTI
comparison carrying far more evidential weight than this one.

## Caveats that belong with the temporal result

- SPAC rain is **synthetic and unphysical in this exact dimension**: rain averages **323 events per exact nanosecond** against background's 8.8, and that trivial statistic alone scores AUC 0.7415.
- The jitter control (fig9, right) shows our density feature is **not** reading that artifact — AUC is flat out to 1 ms of jitter.
- But contrast-maximisation gives a mirror ratio of **1.00–1.07** for SPAC rain versus 6.4–9.6 for genuine translation, so **there is no coherent motion**. The correct reading is *rain is temporally brief, background is persistent* — a claim about accumulation-window choice, not about tracking streaks.
- The `TemporalFrontend` pools energy over t and is therefore **exactly invariant to a circular roll of the sub-windows** (verified, `max|Δ| = 0`), so it structurally cannot read absolute clock phase — the artifact that inflated every absolute-bin probe.
