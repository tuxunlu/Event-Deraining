Pipeline and architecture

What happens to an event between arriving at the sensor and being kept or dropped — every stage, every tensor shape, and where the weights sit.

1 · The inference path

Events arrive as a continuous stream. They are accumulated into a window, binned onto a fixed grid, passed through a dense convolutional trunk, and then either read off directly or refined per event.

Why the grid is fixed Binning to a constant 256×256×16 is what makes the trunk's cost independent of how hard it is raining. A storm producing 900K events per window costs the trunk the same 6.3 ms as a light one at 100K — only the host-to-device copy grows. A point-based model has no such property.

2 · What the per-event head receives

Exit B samples the trunk at each event's exact position and hands it to a small MLP together with features computed directly from the raw event neighbourhood.

The head is a residual correction: it outputs trunk_logit + δ with the final layer zero-initialised, so at the first training step it reproduces the trunk exactly and can only improve from there. The trunk logit is also re-injected at every layer, so the correction never loses sight of what it is correcting.

3 · Inside the trunk

Only two components in each block are rain-specific: the oriented atom bank (32 weights) and the gate that decides how much of each band to suppress (1,112). The FFN that follows is 6,832 — three quarters of the block, doing nothing specific to rain.

What the ungated residual does not guarantee The frontend's residual band carries the part of the signal that never changes, and nothing in the frontend attenuates it. We previously described that as a guarantee that the network cannot delete the static scene. It is not. Trained adversarially to erase a fully static input, the shipped network suppresses it 530×; deleting the residual band entirely moves that to 363×, i.e. not in the direction the claim predicts, so the band was contributing none of the protection. Attacked on its own the decomposition really is immovable (1.0×) — it is ungated where it is defined, and then freely overridden by the 28K weights downstream. A plain CNN with no structure at all reaches 1332×. The full-network figure varies roughly 400–700× across seeds; the ordering does not.

Making it hold

A bypass cannot fix this: out = z + res leaves z unbounded, so the learnable branch cancels the bypass exactly. Bounding the branch does fix it. ORSPNet3DGuard replaces the output with

logit = α·res + C·tanh(z / C)

whose second term is confined to (−C, +C) by construction, so logit > α − C on any fully persistent cell — for any weights, any objective, any adversary. Both constants are fixed, never learned: a learnable α could anneal to zero and take the guarantee with it. Under the same attack the guarded model suppresses static input 3.9×, and the lowest logit the adversary reaches on a persistent pixel is 2.000 — the predicted floor to three decimals, for a keep-probability of 0.881.

Calibration is the whole game, and the condition is α > C. The residual band is effectively binary on persistent content — measured 0.0 below the 90th percentile and exactly 1.0 above it — so a first attempt at α=1, C=4 put the floor at res > 4, which is unreachable; the guarantee never bound and the guarded model came out worse than the plain one. The ON and OFF residuals must also be combined with max, not a mean: averaging halves the floor whenever one polarity is quiet, which silently voids the property.

The cost is real on both datasets, and larger on the rig than on KITTI (−0.046 vs −0.036 at the strong setting). On the rig it concentrates: the two rain levels the unguarded model already found hardest lose a further 0.10 each, against a median of 0.037 elsewhere. The rig generates rain from fixed nozzles, so some of its rain is persistent rain — indistinguishable from scene structure to this cue, and forcibly retained by the floor. That makes real rig data the adversarial case for this guarantee, not the favourable one we expected.

4 · Supervision — the largest lever in the system

Everything above describes the network. The single biggest improvement in the campaign changed none of it.

The result Rebuilding the target as a count majority over both polarities and retraining the same network took event-DA from 0.7052 to 0.9183. That single change is worth 30× the entire spread across five architectures spanning 28K to 265K weights.

The window dominates everything. At 100K events the full model contributes about a fifth of end-to-end latency; the other four fifths is waiting for the window to fill. If latency is the binding constraint, shortening the window buys far more than shrinking the model — and the trunk's flat cost is what makes that viable.
